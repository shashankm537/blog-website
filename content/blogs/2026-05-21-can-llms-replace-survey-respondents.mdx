## AI Mimics Opinions, But Is It Listening to Everyone?

Imagine asking artificial intelligence to act as thousands of American households and share their thoughts on inflation. While some studies suggest Large Language Models (LLMs) can accurately predict average survey responses, a closer look reveals a significant gap: they might be missing the diversity of individual experiences that real surveys capture.

### The Illusion of Consensus

Recent research indicates that when LLMs are prompted to simulate responses from a large group, they can often match the average sentiment of major economic surveys. For instance, in 2020, when a key survey indicated a median inflation expectation of around 3% a year out, a similarly prompted LLM also produced a 3% median. This uncanny accuracy has led to suggestions that LLMs could offer a cheaper, more frequent alternative to traditional surveys like the Survey of Consumer Expectations (SCE).

### Where the Models Fall Short: The Missing Spectrum

However, the superficial agreement breaks down when we examine the breadth of opinions. While LLMs can nail the average, they tend to cluster responses within a very narrow band. A powerful LLM that accurately predicted the average inflation expectation in 2020, for example, placed the vast majority of its simulated respondents within a tight two-percentage-point range. In stark contrast, actual survey data from the same period showed a much wider spread, with individual responses ranging from significantly negative to quite positive inflation expectations. This suggests that the LLM's simulated population isn't truly diverse; it's more like a single, well-informed voice speaking many times, rather than thousands of unique perspectives.

### The Challenge of True Representation

The core issue lies in capturing the "dispersion" or variety of opinions within a population. Traditional surveys excel at revealing how widely beliefs differ, painting a picture of heterogeneous thoughts and concerns. LLMs, at least in their current implementations for this purpose, seem to consolidate these differences into a singular, albeit accurate, average. This means that while they can inform us about the general trend, they may not provide the granular insights into the range of experiences that policymakers and economists rely on.

### What To Watch

The debate is far from settled. Researchers are now exploring ways to "unlearn" LLMs to encourage a broader range of simulated responses, potentially bridging the gap between AI-generated data and real-world heterogeneity. The focus will be on whether these AI models can be fine-tuned to reflect not just the average sentiment, but also the full spectrum of individual beliefs and anxieties.
