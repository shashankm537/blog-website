## AI's Disturbing Echo: Dead Pilots' Voices Recreated, NTSB Halts Public Access

In a stark illustration of artificial intelligence's rapidly expanding capabilities, the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) has temporarily locked down its public investigation database after discovering that the voices of pilots lost in a tragic UPS crash were resurrected using AI. This unprecedented event has raised serious ethical questions about data privacy and the future of accident investigations.

### The Spectrogram's Secret

The NTSB maintains a public docket system, a treasure trove of information for accident investigations, with one crucial exception: cockpit voice recordings. Federal law prohibits their inclusion. However, in the case of the fatal UPS Flight 2976 crash in Louisville last year, the investigation's docket contained a spectrogram file. This file, a visual representation of sound data, essentially translates audio signals into an image, capturing everything from low to high frequencies.

A sharp-eyed observer, popular YouTuber Scott Manley, pointed out on social media that the vast amount of data encoded within this image could potentially be used to reconstruct the original audio. It appears this observation wasn't merely theoretical, as individuals subsequently used this spectrogram, combined with publicly available transcripts of the flight, to generate approximations of the cockpit voice recordings. AI tools, notably Codex, were reportedly employed in this process, leading to eerily recreated voices of the deceased pilots.

### NTSB Responds to AI's Invasion

In light of this concerning development, the NTSB took swift action, temporarily disabling public access to its docket system. While public access was restored on Friday, a significant number of investigations, including the one involving UPS Flight 2976, remain closed as the agency conducts a thorough review. This pause reflects the NTSB's commitment to understanding the implications of AI-generated content and ensuring the integrity and privacy of sensitive investigation data moving forward. The incident underscores the urgent need for clear guidelines and ethical frameworks surrounding the use of AI in sensitive contexts.

### What To Watch

Expect the NTSB to implement new security protocols and potentially revise its data release policies to prevent similar re-creations of sensitive audio. The broader aviation and investigative communities will be watching closely for how this incident influences the handling of digital evidence and the ethical boundaries of AI application in accident probes. Discussions around data ownership and public access to investigative materials are likely to intensify.
