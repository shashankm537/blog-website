**Waymo Robotaxis Take a Rain Check in Four Cities Amidst Flooding Woes**

Waymo, the self-driving car giant, has temporarily suspended its robotaxi services in four major cities due to challenges navigating severe weather, specifically heavy rainfall and flooded roadways. This widespread pause comes on the heels of a recent recall aimed at addressing similar issues with their autonomous vehicles.

### Navigating the Deluge: Atlanta's Stalled Robotaxi

The latest incident occurred in Atlanta, Georgia, where one of Waymo's driverless vehicles was observed attempting to drive through a flooded street. Reports indicate the vehicle became stuck for approximately an hour before being safely recovered by the company. In response to this event and the ongoing severe weather, Waymo confirmed it has halted operations in Atlanta, mirroring similar actions taken in San Antonio, Texas, as they work to refine their navigation systems. "Safety is Waymo’s top priority, both for our riders and everyone we share the road with," a company statement explained, acknowledging the vehicle's encounter with a flooded road.

### Texas Experiences Widespread Service Halts

The issues extend to Texas, where Waymo has also paused its robotaxi services in Dallas and Houston. These decisions were made in light of the severe weather conditions impacting the state this week. A Waymo spokesperson clarified that the service interruptions in these two Texas cities were implemented out of an abundance of caution, anticipating the forecast for dangerous weather. This expansion of the service pause, initially reported by Bloomberg News, highlights the company's ongoing struggle to ensure its autonomous fleet can safely operate in all environmental conditions.

### The Software Update and Its Limitations

Previously, Waymo admitted that a complete solution for avoiding flooded areas was not yet finalized when they issued a software recall last week. Instead, the company deployed an update designed to introduce restrictions in locations and at times with a higher probability of encountering flooded or high-speed roadways. This measure was documented by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA). However, the Atlanta incident suggests these precautions were insufficient to prevent the robotaxi from entering a flooded intersection. Waymo noted that the Atlanta storm produced such rapid rainfall that flooding occurred before any official weather warnings were issued by the National Weather Service, complicating the vehicle's ability to anticipate and avoid the hazard.

### What To Watch

Expect Waymo to closely monitor weather forecasts and local conditions in these affected cities. The company will likely continue to refine its software, focusing on more robust flood detection and avoidance protocols. It remains to be seen how quickly Waymo can implement a permanent fix that allows for consistent service across diverse weather scenarios.
