# HDFC Bank Faces Scrutiny Over Rs 45 Crore Interest Payments

HDFC Bank's stock saw a dip of up to 2% on Wednesday, hitting an intraday low of Rs 761, following reports of an internal probe into Rs 45 crore in interest payments. The investigation reportedly centers on payments made to a state government agency shortly before the former chairman's resignation.

## Internal Audit Raises Red Flags

The situation unfolded after an internal audit of the bank's marketing department, covering the first quarter of the fiscal year 2025, flagged suspicious payments totaling Rs 45 crore. This audit also classified the department's performance as "unsatisfactory," prompting the bank's Audit Committee to initiate a formal "Internal Vigilance Investigation." The payments in question were allegedly made to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation (MSRDC), a government entity.

According to reports, these payments were designated as "differential interest" – interest paid above a specified rate on deposits held by MSRDC. However, instead of being directly transferred to MSRDC as income, the funds were reportedly channeled through the bank's marketing department. These amounts were then allegedly presented as contributions towards a road safety awareness campaign, with funds passing through four local vendors.

## Senior Management Involvement Alleged

Internal records reviewed as part of the probe suggest that senior management, including Chief Executive Officer Sashidhar Jagdishan, was aware of and involved in the decision-making process. Testimonies from bank officials indicate that Jagdishan participated in discussions concerning how to compensate MSRDC and agreed to route the differential interest through the marketing budget as a one-time measure. The bank's Chief Marketing Officer, Ravi Santhanam, reportedly acknowledged during the vigilance probe that the marketing department acted as a "facilitator to camouflage differential interest reimbursement as marketing spend." These developments occurred just days before former chairman Atanu Chakraborty resigned on March 18th.

## What to Watch

Investors and analysts will be closely monitoring the findings of the internal vigilance investigation. The bank's communication regarding the outcome and any potential remedial actions will be crucial in shaping market sentiment. Further details on the role of senior management and the exact nature of the marketing department's involvement are also expected to emerge.
