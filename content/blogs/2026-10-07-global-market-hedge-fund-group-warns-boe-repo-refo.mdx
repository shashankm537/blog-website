## Hedge Funds Sound Alarm Over Bank of England's Repo Market Overhaul

A prominent industry group representing hedge funds has issued a stark warning to the Bank of England, suggesting that proposed reforms to a crucial market for short-term borrowing could inadvertently exacerbate financial instability. The changes, aimed at strengthening the market for loans backed by government debt, could ironically leave investors more vulnerable during times of financial turbulence.

### Concerns Over Central Clearing Expansion

The Alternative Investment Management Association (AIMA), a leading voice for the hedge fund industry, has voiced significant apprehension regarding the Bank of England's plans to expand central clearing within the gilt repurchase (repo) agreement market. In a letter to the central bank, AIMA argues that these proposed shifts could introduce unforeseen vulnerabilities, potentially leaving market participants more exposed to disruptions in funding markets. This represents a more pointed rebuttal from AIMA, following their substantial structural concerns raised in response to the BoE's initial proposals last year.

These warnings emerge against a backdrop of a global bond market selloff, which has sent borrowing costs surging upwards. In the UK specifically, yields on 30-year government bonds have reached their highest point since 1998, underscoring renewed anxieties about the liquidity and stability of government debt markets. Regulators are also keenly observing the increasing influence of hedge funds in sovereign bond markets, with the International Monetary Fund noting their growing presence and the BoE itself having previously highlighted the added risks posed by the heightened activity of leveraged hedge funds in the gilt market.

### Reassessing Risk and Resilience

The Bank of England's proposed measures are intended to bolster the resilience of the gilt repo market, with an emphasis on expanding central clearing. This system involves a central counterparty acting as an intermediary, guaranteeing trades between buyers and sellers, thereby mitigating the risk of one party defaulting. However, AIMA's critique suggests that the proposed expansion might not fully account for the complexities and potential unintended consequences of such a significant structural change in a vital market. The plans also include implementing minimum haircuts on repo transactions that are not centrally cleared, which are essentially discounts designed to provide greater protection for lenders and reduce the likelihood of forced asset sales.

### What To Watch

Investors and market participants will be closely monitoring the Bank of England's response to AIMA's concerns. The central bank's ultimate decision on the repo market reforms will likely be shaped by the balance between its stated goals of enhancing market resilience and the potential risks identified by the hedge fund industry. Future developments in the gilt market, particularly during periods of increased volatility, will offer crucial insights into the effectiveness of these proposed changes.
