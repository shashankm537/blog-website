## Startup Accuses Apple of Contact Sharing Tech Theft in India

A U.S.-based tech startup has launched a significant legal battle against Apple in India, accusing the iPhone giant of infringing on its patented technology with the popular NameDrop feature. The startup, GG Technologies, claims Apple's contact-sharing innovation, which allows users to swap details by simply bringing their devices near each other, is a direct copy of their own patented system.

### The Heart of the Dispute: NameDrop vs. StayTouch

At the center of this high-stakes lawsuit is the NameDrop functionality, introduced by Apple with iOS 17, allowing seamless exchange of contact information between iPhones and compatible Apple Watches. GG Technologies, the company behind the contact-sharing app StayTouch, asserts that Apple's implementation directly mirrors the technology for which they hold a patent. StayTouch, launched in 2019, was designed with the exact same purpose: enabling effortless contact sharing between nearby phones without the need for manual pairing.

### A Global Legal Offensive and Substantial Damages

GG Technologies isn't just focusing on India; the company has also pursued legal action against Apple in the United States over similar patent infringement claims. In India, the startup has filed a substantial claim, seeking approximately $161.86 million in damages and a court order to halt Apple's use of the NameDrop feature. This legal maneuver in India comes after mediation efforts failed to yield a resolution. The company's legal filing in the Bombay High Court is an extensive 781 pages, detailing its alleged grievances.

### What To Watch

With the case yet to be formally listed for a hearing in India, all eyes are on the Bombay High Court. GG Technologies is pushing for an injunction to prevent Apple from selling or importing iPhones and Apple Watches that come preloaded with the contested NameDrop feature into the Indian market. Meanwhile, in the U.S., the International Trade Commission has already opened an investigation into Apple's alleged patent infringement, signaling that this legal saga is likely to unfold across multiple jurisdictions.
