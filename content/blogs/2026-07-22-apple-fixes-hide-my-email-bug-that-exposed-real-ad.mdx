## Apple Patches Privacy Glitch: Your Real Email Was Almost Exposed

For users relying on Apple's "Hide My Email" feature for enhanced privacy, a significant security vulnerability has finally been addressed. The service, designed to shield your personal inbox from spam and unwanted attention, inadvertently exposed real email addresses for a period, potentially undermining its core promise of anonymity.

### The Flaw That Undermined Privacy

The "Hide My Email" service, a premium feature within iCloud+, creates unique, random email addresses that automatically forward to your actual inbox. This innovation aims to give users disposable email addresses, acting as a crucial barrier against spam and phishing attempts. However, a recently patched bug meant that simply sending a message to a "Hide My Email" address that was subsequently rejected as spam could inadvertently reveal the user's true email address within email logs. This meant that even legitimate messages, if flagged incorrectly by spam filters, could have led to the exposure of private information.

### A Long Road to Resolution

This privacy loophole remained unaddressed by Apple for over a year after its initial discovery. A security researcher first alerted Apple to the issue in June 2025. Despite attempts to fix the problem in March and again in late June of 2026, a permanent solution was only deployed on July 3, 2026. The specifics of the exploit were kept under wraps to prevent malicious actors from capitalizing on it, but now that a fix is in place, more details have emerged about the technical nature of the vulnerability. Experts note that it's impossible to know how widespread the exposure was, as affected emails, if rejected as spam, likely never reached users' inboxes for them to discover.

### What To Watch

While Apple has now closed this specific vulnerability, the incident serves as a reminder of the complexities of online privacy. Users who created "Hide My Email" addresses before the July 7, 2026, fix should remain vigilant. Future updates from Apple will likely focus on fortifying its privacy features and ensuring more robust testing before deployment. The company may also provide more transparency regarding past data handling practices related to this incident.
