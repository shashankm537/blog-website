## Freshman Year Surprise: How a Stanford Student Uncovered a Campus Scandal and Penned a Bestseller

Stanford University is known for launching bright minds into successful careers. But for Theo Baker, his own remarkable career began before his freshman year even concluded. By exposing a major scandal that led to the resignation of the university president, Baker not only earned a prestigious journalism award but is now releasing a book detailing his experiences and Stanford's complex ties to venture capital.

### From Coder to Investigative Journalist

Baker arrived at Stanford with a clear vision: to dive into the world of tech and entrepreneurship. However, a personal connection to his late grandfather, who had a passion for the student newspaper, led Baker to join The Stanford Daily as a way to stay connected to him. What started as a casual hobby quickly transformed into something far more significant.

His initial articles garnered unexpected attention, sparking a flood of tips and leading him to an academic discussion platform called PubPeer. It was there that Baker stumbled upon long-standing suspicions about image irregularities in research co-authored by Stanford's then-president, Marc Tessier-Lavigne. This discovery, made just months into his university journey, marked the beginning of a groundbreaking investigation.

### Beyond the Scandal: A Deeper Look at Stanford's Influence

The fallout from Baker's initial reporting was immense, culminating in President Tessier-Lavigne's resignation and earning Baker a George Polk Award. But his exploration didn't stop there. With graduation on the horizon, Baker is publishing "How to Rule the World," a comprehensive look at his time at Stanford. The book promises to delve into the university's intricate and often problematic relationship with the venture capital industry, a subject that has generated significant pre-publication buzz.

### What To Watch

The release of "How to Rule the World" is expected to spark further discussion about university governance, academic integrity, and the influence of external funding on academic institutions. Readers will likely be keen to see how Baker connects his initial scandal-breaking story to the broader themes of power and influence within a major research university.
