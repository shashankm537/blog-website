## The Shadow AI Surge: How Companies Can Tame Unsanctioned AI Tools

Employees are enthusiastically adopting powerful AI tools to boost their productivity, a trend that's leaving many IT and security teams in the dark. This rapid, unmonitored rise of "shadow AI" presents significant risks to corporate data, forcing organizations to find a balance between innovation and security.

### The Unseen Digital Workforce

It's no secret that employees are leveraging AI to streamline their daily tasks, from drafting emails with AI assistants to optimizing code with AI-powered copilots. What's less known is the sheer volume and variety of these tools. Many employees are using three to five different AI applications daily, often without IT department oversight. A considerable number of these tools gain access to sensitive corporate data through simple login approvals, bypassing traditional security measures that monitor network traffic. This creates a critical blind spot for security teams, who lack visibility into the data these unsanctioned tools can access, including shared drives and internal communications.

### Bridging the Visibility Gap

The challenge lies in the fact that modern AI tools often operate outside the corporate network. Unlike traditional threats that might traverse email servers or network pathways, browser-based AI applications connect directly through user credentials. Gartner reports a significant disconnect, with a vast majority of organizations suspecting or confirming the use of unauthorized AI tools, yet a surprisingly low percentage have formal AI governance policies in place. This creates a growing chasm between the evolving needs of employees and the capabilities of security infrastructure, leaving sensitive information vulnerable.

### A Path Forward: Embracing AI Safely

The solution isn't to ban these productivity-enhancing tools, but to integrate them into a managed framework. Organizations need a proactive strategy that allows security teams to gain the necessary visibility while still empowering employees to utilize the AI advancements they desire. This involves establishing clear processes for AI adoption, ensuring that the tools employees are using are not only effective but also secure. By creating a controlled and transparent environment for AI usage, companies can mitigate risks without stifling innovation.

### What To Watch

Expect to see a growing emphasis on comprehensive AI discovery tools that can identify unsanctioned applications. Companies will likely be investing in robust third-party app auditing, focusing on the permissions granted to AI services. Furthermore, expect a surge in the development and implementation of AI governance policies designed to provide clear guidelines for employee AI usage, aiming to strike a balance between embracing new technologies and safeguarding corporate assets.
