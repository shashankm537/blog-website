## New Passkey Phishing Scheme Targets Microsoft Cloud Accounts, Steals Data

Cybercriminals are escalating their tactics, deploying a sophisticated passkey-themed phishing scheme to compromise Microsoft cloud accounts and pilfer sensitive data. Microsoft has revealed details of two alarming campaigns that leverage both AI-generated content and a clever social engineering ploy to trick users into handing over access.

### The Deception of Executive Impersonation

One of the most concerning attacks involved a massive wave of over a million scam emails sent in early August 2026. Threat actors impersonated CEOs of various companies, targeting accounts payable departments. The goal was to trick finance teams into initiating Automated Clearing House (ACH) transfers for a fabricated annual subscription to ServiceNow. These emails were meticulously crafted, even incorporating AI-generated content to create convincing email templates and personalized messages for recipients. This sophisticated approach aimed to reduce skepticism by layering executive impersonation, vendor branding, and forged invoices with supporting email conversations, creating a unified and believable narrative.

### Passkey Exploitation: A New Frontier

Beyond financial fraud, a second campaign is exploiting the growing adoption of passkeys. Attackers are using passkey-themed phishing messages to gain unauthorized access to Microsoft cloud environments. These messages likely aim to trick users into believing they are verifying or updating their passkey credentials, thereby compromising their accounts. Once inside, these malicious actors can then exfiltrate valuable data, posing a significant risk to businesses and their sensitive information. This marks a concerning evolution, as cybercriminals adapt to newer, more secure authentication methods by targeting the human element of the security chain.

### What To Watch

Microsoft has highlighted the sophisticated nature of these attacks, indicating a growing trend in how threat actors are leveraging advanced techniques like AI and targeting emerging authentication methods. Organizations should prioritize educating their employees about these specific phishing tactics, particularly concerning executive impersonation and passkey-related scams. Expect to see further advancements in both the sophistication of these attacks and the methods designed to combat them.
