## Medicare Embraces AI: A Quiet Revolution in Healthcare Payments

The healthcare landscape is on the cusp of a seismic shift, driven not by a new drug or surgical technique, but by a fundamental change in how Medicare pays for care. A new federal program, designed with artificial intelligence at its core, is set to transform how millions of Americans receive care for chronic conditions, a move that has largely flown under the radar of the wider tech world.

### Rewriting the Rules of Reimbursement

For years, Medicare has operated on a fee-for-service model, essentially paying healthcare providers for the time and services they deliver. This system, however, has struggled to accommodate the potential of AI and technology that can monitor patients, offer proactive support, and coordinate care between appointments. Enter ACCESS, or Advancing Chronic Care with Effective, Scalable Solutions. This ambitious 10-year initiative from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) is designed to reward health outcomes rather than just activities. This means organizations participating in ACCESS will receive consistent payments for managing specific chronic conditions, but the full payment is contingent on patients achieving tangible health improvements, such as better blood pressure control or reduced pain levels.

### AI Gets a Seat at the Healthcare Table

This payment model transformation is precisely what allows for innovative approaches like those pioneered by companies such as Pair Team. Neil Batlivala, CEO of Pair Team, whose company focuses on underserved patient populations, highlights that ACCESS creates the financial pathways for AI-driven interventions that were previously impossible. Imagine AI agents monitoring vital signs between doctor visits, initiating personalized check-ins, or even coordinating essential social support like housing referrals. These crucial, but often unbillable, tasks are now being directly incentivized by Medicare, paving the way for a more proactive, efficient, and ultimately, more effective system of care.

### A Diverse Cohort Poised for Innovation

The inaugural group of 150 participants in the ACCESS program is a testament to the broad potential of this new model. It includes a diverse array of organizations, from cutting-edge AI doctor startups and virtual nutrition therapy providers to companies developing connected devices and wearable technology like Whoop. This signals a significant opportunity for the integration of advanced technologies into mainstream healthcare, potentially improving the lives of individuals managing conditions like diabetes, hypertension, chronic kidney disease, obesity, depression, and anxiety.

### What To Watch

As the ACCESS program officially launches in early July, all eyes will be on how these diverse participants leverage the new payment structure to drive better patient outcomes. The success of this initiative could very well set a precedent for how the federal government approaches technological innovation in other heavily regulated industries, marking a pivotal moment in the fusion of AI and public health.
