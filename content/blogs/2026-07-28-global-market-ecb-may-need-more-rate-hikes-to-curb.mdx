## Inflation’s Stubborn Grip: ECB Hints at More Rate Hikes Amidst Geopolitical Jitters

The European Central Bank (ECB) may be forced to continue its aggressive stance on interest rates, with at least one more hike on the horizon to combat persistent inflation risks. This warning comes from Peter Kazimir, the Slovak central bank chief, who also suggested that escalating economic and geopolitical uncertainties could necessitate even further tightening than currently anticipated by financial markets.

### The Shadow of Rising Energy Prices

Recent surges in oil and gas prices, fueled by renewed tensions in the Middle East, have cast a shadow over the ECB's efforts to tame inflation. While the central bank opted to hold borrowing costs steady at its most recent meeting, signals are emerging that a rate increase could be on the table as early as September. Kazimir, known for his hawkish views within the ECB, believes a measured approach to adjusting interest rates is still paramount. He argues that even a modest improvement in the current economic landscape wouldn't negate the need for another rate hike.

### A Proactive Stance on Inflationary Pressures

For Kazimir, a significant turnaround in upcoming economic data and geopolitical developments would be required to sway his support for holding rates steady at the ECB's September gathering. He emphasized the critical need for policymakers to act preemptively against potential inflationary pressures rather than reacting once they are fully entrenched. The Slovak policymaker pointed out that the ripple effects of higher energy costs, known as second-round effects, can creep in slowly and become considerably more difficult to unwind once they take root.

### Navigating Market Expectations

Financial markets are currently anticipating at least two more rate increases from the ECB, with the first expected by October and a subsequent one by March. However, these projections are highly susceptible to fluctuations in energy prices, particularly crude oil. The central bank's July decision to pause rate hikes was partly attributed to the absence of major second-round inflation effects. Yet, Kazimir advocates for continued vigilance and proactive measures to avert the resurgence of an inflationary cycle.

### What to Watch

All eyes will be on the incoming economic data and the evolving geopolitical landscape. Any further escalation in global tensions or a sustained rise in energy prices will likely solidify expectations for additional rate hikes from the ECB. Market participants will also be closely monitoring speeches and statements from other ECB policymakers for further clues on the future path of monetary policy.
