## Sophisticated Cyberattack Unveiled: Spark RAT Targets Cambodia, Exploiting Security Weaknesses

Cambodia is in the crosshairs of a new, sophisticated cyber threat. Threat actors are deploying an open-source remote access trojan (RAT) known as Spark RAT, specifically targeting individuals and organizations within the country. What makes this campaign particularly alarming is its clever use of a legitimate, yet vulnerable, driver to bypass security measures and gain unfettered access.

### Deceptive Digital Lures for Maximum Impact

This malicious campaign utilizes a wide array of deceptive tactics to ensnare its victims. Researchers have observed the Spark RAT being delivered through compressed files containing seemingly legitimate installers, disguised with enticing file names. These lures range from official-sounding government notices and public health advisories to real estate documents and even promotional offers, indicating a broad approach to captivate a diverse audience. The attackers' goal is to exploit the trust placed in these familiar document types to trick users into executing the malicious payload.

### Exploiting Trust: The Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver Tactic

A key element of this attack is the "bring your own vulnerable driver" (BYOVD) technique. Instead of relying on novel exploits, the attackers leverage a known, legitimate driver from OPSWAT's AppRemover tool, specifically a file named "ardrv.sys." This driver, while intended for legitimate security purposes, contains vulnerabilities that the attackers exploit to elevate their privileges on a compromised system. This allows them to disable or circumvent existing security software, creating a clear path for the Spark RAT to take control.

The attack chain typically begins with phishing emails containing these malicious archives. Once the user is tricked into running the installer, a multi-stage process unfolds. A signed Tencent executable is used for DLL side-loading, which then delivers intermediate payloads. These payloads are crucial for deploying the vulnerable "ardrv.sys" driver and, subsequently, launching the Go-based Spark RAT. This RAT is designed for cross-platform compatibility, meaning it can control a wide range of compromised devices remotely. Adding another layer of evasion, the attack chain includes anti-sandbox checks, designed to detect and evade security analysis environments by manipulating timing mechanisms. It also actively searches for and terminates processes associated with Chinese endpoint security programs like Huorong Internet Security.

### What To Watch

Security researchers will undoubtedly continue to monitor for any further evolution of this campaign, particularly any new lure themes or variations in the attack methodology. Organizations in Cambodia should remain vigilant against phishing attempts and ensure their security software is up-to-date and properly configured, paying close attention to any unusual system behavior or alerts that might indicate a compromise.
