## European Lawmaker Investigating Spyware Falls Victim to Pegasus Attack

A chilling development has emerged in the ongoing saga of digital surveillance: a prominent European politician, who was actively investigating the misuse of sophisticated spyware, has himself been targeted by the very same invasive technology. This revelation, confirmed by leading digital rights researchers, has ignited fresh outrage and cast a stark spotlight on the potential for governments to weaponize surveillance tools against those who seek to hold them accountable.

### A Target Under Investigation

The victim, identified as Stelios Kouloglou, a Greek former journalist and now a Member of the European Parliament, served on the special PEGA committee. This committee was specifically established to examine the alarming trend of European governments employing powerful surveillance software, such as the notorious Pegasus spyware, often allegedly to monitor critics and political opponents. Security experts at The Citizen Lab have definitively linked Kouloglou's phone to Pegasus infections that occurred between 2022 and 2023, marking the first publicly confirmed instance of a lawmaker on this particular investigative body being subjected to such an attack.

### The Unsettling Implications

The compromise of Kouloglou's device is far more than a personal security breach; it's being decried as a direct assault on democratic principles. One fellow European lawmaker described the hacking as "a direct attack on the rule of law," underscoring the gravity of the situation. This incident raises serious questions about who is willing to go to such lengths to silence investigators and potentially influence the outcome of the PEGA committee's crucial report, which is expected to detail widespread abuses. While the exact perpetrator remains unidentified by Citizen Lab, the reuse of a known Pegasus deployment email address suggests a sophisticated and authorized operator.

### A Shadow Over Democratic Oversight

The use of spyware, often justified by governments for national security and combating serious crime, has repeatedly been shown to extend to the surveillance of journalists, activists, and elected officials. The fact that a key investigator into these very abuses was himself compromised highlights the deep-seated challenges in ensuring accountability and transparency in the digital realm. This incident demands a robust response from the European Commission, pushing for stricter regulations and oversight to prevent the erosion of privacy and the chilling of dissent within the EU.

### What To Watch

The coming weeks will likely see intense scrutiny of the PEGA committee's findings and renewed calls for concrete legislative action to curb the unchecked spread and use of spyware across Europe. Expect further analysis from security researchers as they attempt to pinpoint the entity responsible for the attack on Kouloglou, and continued debate within the European Parliament regarding the future of digital surveillance and its ethical boundaries.
