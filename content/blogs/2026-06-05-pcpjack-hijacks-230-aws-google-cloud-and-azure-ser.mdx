# Shadow Operations: Hackers Turn Major Cloud Servers into Secret Email Network

Cybercriminals have been caught red-handed, transforming hundreds of legitimate cloud servers from industry giants like Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure into a clandestine network for sending emails. This sophisticated operation, attributed to a threat actor dubbed "PCPJack," was discovered still actively running, highlighting a significant new avenue for malicious online activity.

## Hijacked Infrastructure Revealed

Researchers stumbled upon a startling discovery: over 230 compromised business servers, spread across the United States, Europe, and Asia, had been quietly repurposed. These servers were meticulously configured to act as anonymous mail relays, effectively masking the origin of vast amounts of email. The operation was so sophisticated that the compromised infrastructure was still functional when detected by security experts, indicating a prolonged and well-executed campaign.

## A Glimpse into the Hacker's Toolkit

The investigation into PCPJack's operation uncovered a treasure trove of data left behind on an unsecured command-and-control server. This included source code, compiled software, system logs, network scanning tools, and even the configuration for a popular hacking tool called Sliver. This accidental exposure provided an unprecedented look into the methods and tools employed by the attackers, revealing a calculated approach to establishing and maintaining their illicit network. The deployment of specialized binaries, disguised as hidden files and designed to communicate with the command-and-control server, demonstrates a deep understanding of cloud environments and evasion techniques.

## What to Watch

The discovery of PCPJack's extensive SMTP relay network raises immediate concerns about the potential for large-scale spam campaigns, phishing attacks, and other illicit communications to be routed through seemingly legitimate cloud infrastructure. Organizations leveraging AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure should remain vigilant for any unusual network activity or signs of compromise. Security researchers will undoubtedly continue to dissect the leaked data, aiming to further understand PCPJack's tactics, techniques, and procedures, and to develop more robust defenses against such sophisticated cloud-based threats.
