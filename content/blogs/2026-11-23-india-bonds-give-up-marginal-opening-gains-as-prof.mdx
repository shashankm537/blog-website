## Indian Bonds Revert Gains as Profit-Taking Dominates

Indian government bonds experienced a reversal of fortune today, shedding earlier gains as investors opted to book profits. The rupee's sharp depreciation also played a significant role in dampening sentiment, pushing the benchmark yield higher during trading.

### Profit-Taking Trumps Optimism

After a promising start to the trading session, the Indian bond market saw a shift as traders began to cash in on recent gains. The benchmark 6.94% 2036 bond, which had seen a notable dip in yield last week, saw its return edge upwards. This profit-taking activity suggests that market participants are wary of holding onto their positions amid ongoing economic uncertainties. The allure of immediate gains proved stronger than the prospect of further appreciation in the short term.

### Rupee's Decline Adds to Pressure

Adding to the downward pressure on bonds was a significant weakening of the Indian rupee. A depreciating currency can make it less attractive for foreign investors to hold Indian debt, as the returns in their home currency are diminished. This currency movement likely prompted some investors to reassess their holdings and consider reducing their exposure to Indian assets. The interplay between bond yields and currency strength is a critical factor for global investors.

### Focus Shifts to RBI Policy

Last week, a combination of falling US Treasury yields and a stronger rupee had offered a reprieve to Indian bonds, pushing yields lower. However, the sentiment appears to have shifted as investors now turn their attention to the upcoming Reserve Bank of India (RBI) policy decision on December 4. The central bank's pronouncements on interest rates and its outlook for the economy will be closely scrutinized for clues about future monetary policy direction. Any hints of a less dovish stance could further influence bond market movements.

### What To Watch

Market participants will be keenly observing the RBI's policy statement and the accompanying press conference for any indications of future interest rate policy and the central bank's assessment of inflation and growth. The trajectory of the Indian rupee will also remain a key factor to monitor, as will global economic developments and US Treasury yield movements.
