## Europe Sounds Alarm on U.S. Chip Restrictions Targeting China

Europe is voicing significant concerns over a proposed U.S. bill that could severely impact its leading technology companies and disrupt global semiconductor supply chains. A high-level Dutch delegation recently journeyed to Washington, urging lawmakers to reconsider measures that could effectively cut off key Chinese chip manufacturers from crucial Western technology.

### Dutch Minister Champions ASML's Concerns

Dutch Trade Minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma made a pointed visit to the U.S. capital this week, engaging in discussions with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and members of Congress. His primary objective was to articulate the profound reservations Europe holds regarding the "MATCH Act." This proposed legislation aims to expand existing restrictions on exporting semiconductor manufacturing equipment to China, a move that would disproportionately affect ASML, a crown jewel of the Dutch and European tech sector.

ASML, a company headquartered in the Netherlands, stands as Europe's most valuable technology firm and holds a unique position as the world's sole producer of the highly specialized lithography machines essential for creating advanced AI chips. Minister Sjoerdsma emphasized the gravity of the situation, stating, "It’s exceptional that I’m coming here to broadly outline our concerns to Congress." He underscored the significant risks involved, noting, "The stakes for the Netherlands may be very high."

### Broadening Restrictions, Deeper Impact

The proposed MATCH Act would represent a substantial escalation of current U.S. export controls. While existing regulations already prohibit the sale of ASML's most advanced Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography tools to China, the new bill seeks to extend these restrictions to include ASML's deep ultraviolet immersion machines. These machines, though not the absolute cutting edge, are still vital for producing a wide range of semiconductors.

As ASML's CEO Christophe Fouquet previously explained, China currently procures older-generation deep ultraviolet tools from the company, equipment that has been in production for about a decade. The MATCH Act, introduced in April, would effectively classify these now older but still functional machines as off-limits, significantly curtailing China's ability to upgrade its domestic chip manufacturing capabilities. This expansion of controls could directly impact ASML's revenue streams, as China currently accounts for a notable 19% of its net system sales.

### What To Watch

The MATCH Act has yet to undergo a full vote in either the U.S. House or Senate, with reports suggesting it may need to be incorporated into a larger legislative package to gain traction. European allies, alongside U.S. tech giants, will be closely monitoring legislative developments as they weigh the geopolitical implications of these chip war escalations against the economic realities of global technology interdependence. The outcome will significantly shape the future of semiconductor innovation and international trade.
