## Tech Giant Puts $1 Million Price Tag on Hackers After Major Breach

In a dramatic escalation following a significant cyberattack, Japanese tech conglomerate GMO Internet Group is now offering a substantial reward to help bring the perpetrators to justice. The company is dangling up to $1 million for any information that leads to the capture of the individuals responsible for breaching its Onamae.com Desktop Cloud service, a platform critical for foreign exchange trading operations. This bold move comes after GMO refused to meet a staggering $50 million ransom demand from the attackers.

### Unmasking the Cybercriminals

The breach, which compromised customer information, sent shockwaves through the financial trading community. The compromised Onamae.com Desktop Cloud service provides virtual Windows environments essential for FX traders. Investigations have already linked unauthorized trades on some customer accounts to credentials leaked from this very service, highlighting the direct financial impact of the attack. The attackers, believing they are GMO, made their hefty extortion demand on October 21st, and ominously threatened the trading accounts and sensitive data of GMO's clients.

### The Bounty Hunt Begins

GMO has officially launched its "GMO Cybercrime Bounty" program, actively soliciting tips from the public. The company is seeking crucial intelligence that connects individuals, servers, domains, IP addresses, accounts, or cryptocurrency wallets to the attack, the subsequent extortion attempt, or the movement of illicit funds. Importantly, the reward is contingent on actionable intelligence that directly contributes to an arrest or the issuance of an international wanted notice. This ensures that only genuine leads that advance the investigation will be rewarded, with the total bounty potentially awarded to a single tipster or distributed among multiple contributors depending on their impact. The program remains open for submissions until an arrest is made, an international wanted notice is issued, or until October 27, 2027, whichever comes first.

### What to Watch

The cybersecurity world will be closely watching the effectiveness of this unusual bounty program. Will the substantial reward incentivize the right kind of information to surface, or will the attackers prove too elusive? Furthermore, the ongoing fallout for GMO's clients and the potential for further repercussions from the leaked data remain significant concerns. The company's proactive stance, while costly, signals a firm commitment to combating cybercrime and protecting its users.
