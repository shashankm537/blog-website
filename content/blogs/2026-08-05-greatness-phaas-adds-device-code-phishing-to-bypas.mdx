## Phishing-as-a-Service Goes Advanced: Greatness Toolkit Now Exploits Device Code Phishing to Evade MFA

Cybercriminals are constantly finding new ways to compromise sensitive accounts, and a popular phishing toolkit known as Greatness has just upgraded its arsenal. This sophisticated platform is now incorporating a dangerous technique called "device code phishing," allowing attackers to bypass crucial Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) and steal valuable session tokens. This development signals a worrying trend of phishing services evolving into comprehensive attack platforms.

### The Rise of Device Code Phishing

Device code phishing is a relatively new but rapidly growing threat that cleverly exploits a legitimate process used by many popular services like Microsoft, Google, and Apple. Normally, this feature allows users to easily sign into a device (like a smart TV or a gaming console) by entering a short code on a separate computer or phone. Attackers are now abusing this process to trick victims into unknowingly authorizing access to their accounts, effectively sidestepping MFA protections that were designed to keep them safe.

### Greatness Evolves into an Integrated Attack Ecosystem

The Greatness toolkit, first identified by cybersecurity researchers last year, has now broadened its capabilities beyond simple credential harvesting. It now offers adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) attacks, where attackers can steal both login details and session tokens, alongside the newly added device code phishing and OAuth consent abuse. This integrated approach, managed through a single dashboard, makes it easier for less technically skilled individuals to launch sophisticated phishing campaigns targeting major platforms like iCloud, Yahoo, and Google Workspace.

Access to Greatness is provided via a subscription model on Telegram, with prices starting at $289 per month. This subscription grants users access to a comprehensive operator panel that includes campaign analytics, domain management, and a variety of enticing lure templates designed to trick victims into clicking malicious links or providing their information. These templates often mimic common notifications like voicemails or document sharing requests, further increasing their effectiveness.

### What To Watch

The increasing sophistication and accessibility of phishing-as-a-service platforms like Greatness highlight the ongoing cat-and-mouse game in cybersecurity. Users should remain vigilant and critically examine any requests to enter codes or authorize devices, even if they appear to come from legitimate services. Organizations will likely need to explore advanced security measures and user education to counter these evolving threats.
