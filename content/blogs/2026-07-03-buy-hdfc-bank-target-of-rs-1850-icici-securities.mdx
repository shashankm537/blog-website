## HDFC Bank: ICICI Securities Upgrades to "Buy" with Rs 1,850 Target

Investors are taking note as ICICI Securities has issued a strong "Buy" recommendation for HDFC Bank, setting an ambitious price target of Rs 1,850. This move comes after a thorough analysis of the bank's performance and strategic positioning following its recent significant merger. The report, released on April 21, 2024, suggests a positive outlook for the financial giant.

### Navigating Post-Merger Dynamics

The integration of HDFC Bank has brought about notable shifts in its financial structure. Analysts highlight a significant increase in the Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR) and a reduction in the share of low-cost Current Account Savings Account (CASA) deposits, offset by a rise in borrowings. Despite these adjustments, the merger has boosted the bank's loan market share considerably, although its deposit market share growth has been more measured. ICICI Securities projects robust annual deposit growth, anticipating trillions in absolute terms, even amidst evolving market competition. The bank is expected to prioritize a stable LDR, potentially moderating loan growth while strategically managing its borrowings.

### Earnings Outlook and Stock Valuation

While the recent Net Interest Margin (NIM) figures for the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024 were slightly more encouraging than anticipated, they may have been influenced by seasonal factors. Consequently, ICICI Securities has slightly adjusted its Earnings Per Share (EPS) estimates downwards for the upcoming fiscal years, reflecting the recalibrated growth projections. Nevertheless, the outlook remains positive, with expectations of healthy Return on Assets (RoA) and Return on Equity (RoE) figures for FY25 and FY26. The brokerage firm maintains its Rs 1,850 price target, and a recent dip in the stock price has prompted the upgrade to "Buy."

### What To Watch

Investors will be closely monitoring HDFC Bank's progress in balancing its loan and deposit growth, as well as its success in managing its borrowing costs. The pace at which credit growth aligns with overall system growth and the gradual nature of borrowing substitution will be key factors influencing the stock's re-rating. The market will also be keen to see how the bank's NIMs perform in the coming quarters as the full impact of the merger continues to unfold.
