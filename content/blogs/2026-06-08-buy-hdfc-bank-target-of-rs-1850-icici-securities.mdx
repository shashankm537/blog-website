## HDFC Bank: ICICI Securities Eyes 15% Upside Amid Post-Merger Adjustments

HDFC Bank is back on the radar for investors, with a leading brokerage firm initiating a "Buy" recommendation. Following a significant merger, the bank is undergoing strategic adjustments, and analysts believe these changes position it for future growth, setting a new target price.

### Navigating the Post-Merger Landscape

The recent amalgamation has brought about substantial shifts in HDFC Bank's financial structure. Notably, there's been a notable increase in the loan-to-deposit ratio (LDR) and a dip in the share of low-cost current account savings account (CASA) deposits, accompanied by a rise in overall borrowing. While the merger boosted the bank's loan market share considerably, its deposit growth has been more measured. However, analysts project robust deposit growth in the coming years, with estimates suggesting an annual increase of ₹4 to ₹5 trillion, even considering a more competitive banking environment.

### Strategic Loan Growth and Profitability Projections

Despite strong deposit accretion, HDFC Bank is expected to prioritize a balanced LDR and manage its borrowing mix. This strategic approach leads to adjusted loan growth forecasts, with a projected compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 13% for FY24-26, and an anticipated easing of the LDR in the upcoming fiscal years. The bank's net interest margin (NIM) performance, while showing some seasonal strength in the last quarter, is a key factor to watch. Analysts have slightly trimmed earnings per share (EPS) estimates for FY25 and FY26 due to these growth adjustments, but still anticipate healthy return on assets (RoA) and return on equity (RoE) in the mid-to-high teens.

### What To Watch

With a target price of ₹1,850, the brokerage's upgrade to "Buy" comes after a recent dip in the stock's price. Investors will be keen to observe how effectively HDFC Bank balances its credit and deposit growth, and whether its borrowing strategy translates into sustained profitability. The gradual nature of these strategic shifts suggests a progressive re-rating for the stock in the near to medium term.
