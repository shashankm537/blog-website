North Korean threat actors have unleashed a sophisticated digital assault, publishing over 100 malicious software packages and browser extensions across popular development platforms. This ongoing campaign, dubbed "PolinRider," is a significant expansion of a broader espionage effort known as "Contagious Interview," which targets software developers and cryptocurrency professionals with deceptive job offers. The activity represents a worrying escalation in supply chain attacks, potentially compromising thousands of open-source projects and their users.

### A Flood of Malicious Code

Security researchers have identified 108 unique malicious packages and web browser extensions distributed across npm, Packagist, Go, and Google Chrome. These digital threats are part of the active PolinRider campaign, where threat actors systematically compromise legitimate maintainer accounts, modify existing repositories, and then publish infected versions of popular software. According to security expert Karlo Zanki, new malicious packages are expected to continue appearing as the attackers exploit their access. This widespread infiltration includes 19 npm libraries, 10 Composer packages, 61 Go modules, and a Google Chrome extension, making it a diverse and far-reaching attack on the software supply chain.

### The Deceptive "Contagious Interview" Campaign

PolinRider is merely one facet of a larger, North Korea-aligned operation dubbed "Contagious Interview," which has been active since at least 2023. This overarching campaign meticulously targets software developers and individuals in the cryptocurrency sector. Attackers cunningly impersonate recruiters or collaborators on platforms like LinkedIn, GitHub, and freelance websites. They often go to great lengths to establish trust, creating elaborate front companies and even using AI-generated employee profiles to present a convincing facade. The ultimate goal is to trick unsuspecting professionals into executing malicious code, frequently a new variant of a known JavaScript malware called BeaverTail.

### Mounting Compromises on GitHub

The impact of this campaign is particularly pronounced on GitHub, a cornerstone for open-source development. By April 11, 2026, the activity had compromised a staggering 1,951 public GitHub repositories belonging to 1,047 unique owners. The OpenSourceMalware team first flagged PolinRider in March 2026, noting the threat actors' method of implanting malicious, obfuscated JavaScript payloads into hundreds of public GitHub repositories. Adding another layer of complexity, this campaign has also merged with a cluster known as TaskJacker, which further compromises GitHub users by dropping malicious VS Code task files into their existing projects, extending the potential for backdoor access and data theft.

### What To Watch

As the "PolinRider" campaign remains active, developers and organizations must exercise extreme vigilance. Expect security researchers to continue monitoring and reporting on new malicious packages and compromised repositories. Users of npm, Packagist, Go, and Chrome extensions should prioritize verifying the authenticity of software sources and maintaining up-to-date security practices. Furthermore, professionals in the tech and crypto sectors should remain highly skeptical of unsolicited job offers or collaboration requests, thoroughly vetting potential employers and partners to avoid falling victim to these sophisticated social engineering tactics.
