## Harvard Goes Digital: AI Instructors Now Offer Feedback in Entrepreneurship Bootcamp

Harvard Business School is stepping into the future of education, leveraging cutting-edge AI to enhance its entrepreneurship program. The renowned institution is now incorporating AI-generated avatars of its instructors to provide personalized feedback to participants in its new HBS Foundry bootcamp, aiming to broaden its educational reach.

### The Digital Mentor Experience

The eight-week HBS Foundry program, priced at $699, combines live instruction with a novel AI feedback system. While real instructors lead weekly sessions, it's their AI counterparts who are tasked with critiquing student practice pitches and simulated board meetings. These digital doppelgangers, brought to life by the startup HeyGen, aim to offer a consistent and scalable way to deliver individualized guidance. Project director Katharina Rings shared that the initial concept leaned more towards a chatbot, but student feedback from a trial run indicated a desire for a more interactive and guided experience, leading to the development of the avatars.

### Real-World Reactions to the Virtual Coach

The effectiveness and reception of these AI instructors have already been put to the test. New York Times reporter Sarah Kessler, a participant in the program, shared her experience pitching a rather unusual idea – "Uber for bananas" – to an AI replica of Flybridge Capital co-founder Jeff Bussgang. Both the real Bussgang and his digital twin reportedly offered less-than-enthusiastic responses to the concept. However, Kessler noted a distinct difference in their delivery, observing a "noticeably frozen smile" from the virtual instructor during her pitch. Despite the initial novelty and a touch of the uncanny, participants in the Foundry program have responded positively to the avatars, finding the guided experience valuable. Even the real Jeff Bussgang admitted his digital version felt a bit "creepy," but conceded, "My students love it."

### What To Watch

As Harvard continues to explore the integration of AI in education, the success and scalability of this avatar-driven feedback model will be closely observed. Future iterations may see more sophisticated AI interactions and potentially broader application across other HBS programs. The program's reception by a wider range of students will also be key in determining the long-term viability of this innovative teaching approach.
