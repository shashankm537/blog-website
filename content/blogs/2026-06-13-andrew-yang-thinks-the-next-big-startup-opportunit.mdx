## Andrew Yang's New Mission: Building Startups That Save You Money

Forget the next groundbreaking app or revolutionary gadget. Former presidential candidate and entrepreneur Andrew Yang believes the real innovation for the coming years will come from businesses designed to *give* money back to consumers, not just take it. He's pointing to everyday necessities like housing, food, and communication as prime targets for this cost-saving revolution.

### The "Cost Plus" Inspiration

Yang's inspiration for this new venture comes from an unlikely source: billionaire Mark Cuban's pharmacy startup, Cost Plus Drugs. Cuban's company revolutionized drug pricing by selling medication at cost, a move that deeply resonated with Yang. This sparked a broader idea: what if startups could fundamentally change how we pay for essential goods and services, focusing on returning value to the customer instead of maximizing profit extraction?

He's identified key areas where this model could thrive, including housing, education, groceries, and mobile phone plans. Yang argues that as technology like AI reshapes the job market and potentially suppresses wages, finding ways to reduce the cost of living will become paramount for most Americans. This isn't just a business idea; it's a response to a looming economic challenge.

### Launching a Mobile Revolution

Taking his own advice, Yang has already launched a venture in this space: Noble Mobile. This new mobile virtual network operator aims to significantly undercut the prices of traditional carriers, offering cell service at a fraction of the cost. Even more strikingly, Noble Mobile offers customers a rebate if they use less data, directly aligning the company's success with the customer's savings.

This move reflects Yang's long-held belief that the benefits of technological advancements, particularly from AI, need to be shared more broadly. His previous advocacy for Universal Basic Income (UBI) stemmed from a similar concern about AI's potential to displace workers and concentrate wealth. While his presidential campaign didn't achieve its ultimate goal, Yang maintains that the core premise – redistributing value to everyday citizens – is more relevant than ever.

### What To Watch

Expect to see more companies embracing this "customer-first" profit model, focusing on areas like affordable housing solutions, accessible education platforms, and cost-effective grocery delivery. Yang's Noble Mobile is just the beginning of what could be a significant shift in how startups approach their value proposition. The focus will likely be on measurable savings for consumers, challenging established industries with a more equitable approach.
