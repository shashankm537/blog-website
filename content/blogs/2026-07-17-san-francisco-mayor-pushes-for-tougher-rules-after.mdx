## Robotaxis Grinding Gears: San Francisco Mayor Demands Stricter Self-Driving Car Rules

San Francisco's mayor, Daniel Lurie, once a vocal proponent of the city as a tech innovation hub, is now drawing a line in the sand. Following a crippling traffic jam caused by stalled autonomous vehicles on a busy July 4th, Lurie is urging state regulators to implement more robust rules for self-driving car operations, particularly during significant public events.

### The July 4th Fiasco and a Mayor's Frustration

The city's frustration boiled over after Waymo robotaxis, caught in the surge of holiday traffic and a subsequent power outage, became immobile across key San Francisco streets. This wasn't an isolated incident; a similar situation unfolded during a widespread power outage in December. Both events highlighted a critical flaw in the current system: autonomous vehicles struggling to cope with extraordinary circumstances. The July 4th gridlock not only affected thousands of commuters but also snarled municipal shuttle services, exacerbating the citywide standstill.

### A Call for Enhanced Operational Capabilities

In a letter to the California Department of Transportation, Mayor Lurie emphasized that the current regulatory framework fails to adequately address how autonomous vehicles perform during unplanned or large-scale incidents. He argues that the focus needs to shift from merely ensuring safety in normal conditions to guaranteeing reliability during extraordinary ones. Lurie has proposed four core operational capabilities that self-driving car manufacturers should be required to demonstrate. These include the ability to promptly clear active travel lanes when disabled, adapt routes and service areas in real-time to changing conditions, and share live operational data with local authorities.

### What To Watch

San Francisco's push for stricter regulations signifies a potential turning point for autonomous vehicle deployment in urban environments. Expect continued debate and scrutiny from both tech companies and regulatory bodies. The state's response to Mayor Lurie's proposals will be crucial in shaping the future of robotaxi operations and their integration into city infrastructure, especially during peak events and emergencies.
