## The Unspoken VC Rule: Is AI Making It Impossible to Avoid Backing Your Rivals?

For years, venture capital operated under an unspoken pact: a firm wouldn't invest in two companies directly competing against each other. But the current AI gold rush is pushing this long-standing principle to its breaking point, with even storied firms like Benchmark admitting that the old rules are becoming increasingly difficult to uphold.

### The AI Arms Race and Investor Dilemmas

The explosive growth in artificial intelligence has created a unique challenge for investors. We've seen major AI players like OpenAI and Anthropic attracting investment from many of the same venture capital firms, blurring the lines of direct competition. More recently, a public dispute between AI startups Factory and Cognition highlighted the tensions. Factory accused a former board observer and advisor, Chris Degnan, of unethical conduct, alleging he shared confidential information while transitioning to a rival company, Cognition. This incident, amplified by public commentary from prominent investor Vinod Khosla, who backs both firms, underscores the complex web of relationships and potential conflicts emerging in the AI space.

### Benchmark's Acknowledgment of a Changing Landscape

Benchmark, a firm renowned for its long-term, founder-centric approach, has publicly acknowledged that the traditional avoidance of backing competitors is no longer a guaranteed strategy in the face of AI's rapid evolution. At a recent TechCrunch Disrupt event, Benchmark partners indicated that conflicts arising from portfolio companies expanding into overlapping markets are becoming a necessary consequence of the current industry dynamics. Partner Eric Vishria stated that the rapid pace of change means companies will inevitably pivot and evolve, leading to unavoidable clashes. While Benchmark maintains its commitment to founder confidentiality, Vishria suggested that the practical impact of any shared knowledge is limited, as VCs are not typically deep technical experts.

### What To Watch

The ongoing proliferation of AI startups and the rapid advancements in the field suggest that these investor-dilemmas will persist and likely intensify. Investors will need to navigate increasingly intricate portfolio dynamics, and founders may need to prepare for the possibility that their backers could also be supporting direct rivals. The industry will be watching closely to see how venture capital firms adapt their strategies to balance fostering innovation with managing potential conflicts of interest.
