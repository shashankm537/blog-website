## Walmart's Flipkart Gears Up to Disrupt India's Food Delivery Landscape

In a move set to shake up India's booming online food delivery market, e-commerce giant Flipkart, backed by retail behemoth Walmart, is quietly testing its own food delivery service. This strategic foray aims to challenge the established dominance of industry leaders Zomato and Swiggy in a market that has historically proven challenging for even tech giants like Amazon and Google.

### A Calculated Culinary Push

Flipkart's ambition in the food delivery space is becoming increasingly evident. Sources indicate that the company has been running internal trials, expanding its employee-only service from a few hundred participants to an impressive 8,000. This careful scaling suggests a robust preparation for a broader public launch. Furthermore, Flipkart is leveraging its existing logistics arm, Ekart, by deploying a dedicated fleet of riders specifically for food deliveries, distinct from their quick-commerce operations. This dual-pronged approach highlights a serious commitment to building a reliable and efficient delivery network.

### Tapping into New Avenues and Existing Strengths

The testing phase reveals Flipkart's innovative strategy, integrating a dedicated food delivery tab within its main shopping app, positioned alongside popular offerings like quick-commerce and travel. This integrated approach aims to capture existing user attention. Significantly, Flipkart is utilizing ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce), a government initiative designed to foster competition by connecting buyers and sellers across various platforms. This, coupled with direct restaurant partnerships, provides a flexible and potentially cost-effective method for onboarding a diverse range of culinary options. Riders have already been spotted in Bengaluru sporting the "Eat-in by Minutes" branding, signaling that the service is actively in motion on the ground.

### The Competitive Feast Ahead

Flipkart enters a market brimming with potential, with estimates suggesting an annual growth rate of 18% to 20% for online food delivery in India. Despite this rapid expansion, online ordering still represents just over 15% of the overall food services industry. The current duopoly of Zomato and Swiggy serves a substantial customer base, but the sheer size of India's internet-connected population means there's ample room for new players to carve out a significant share. Flipkart's move is part of a larger trend of innovation in the sector, with companies like Rapido also exploring food delivery services.

### What To Watch

As Flipkart refines its "Eat-in by Minutes" offering, all eyes will be on its official public launch date and its strategy for acquiring customers and restaurants. The company's ability to leverage its existing user base and Ekart's logistics infrastructure will be crucial in its bid to gain traction against the well-entrenched Zomato and Swiggy. The success of Flipkart's venture could also pave the way for further consolidation and innovation in India's dynamic food tech landscape.
