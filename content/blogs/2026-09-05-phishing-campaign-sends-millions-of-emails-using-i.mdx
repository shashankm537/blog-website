# Invisible Trickery: Phishing Campaigns Deploy Hidden Characters to Fool Filters

Cybercriminals are employing a sophisticated new tactic, embedding invisible characters within emails to bypass spam and phishing filters, Microsoft has revealed. This novel approach, dubbed "ASCII smuggling," is being used in high-volume campaigns, signaling a concerning evolution in how threat actors operate in our increasingly AI-driven digital landscape.

## The Art of Hiding in Plain Sight

Attackers are leveraging a specific set of Unicode characters that, while invisible to the human eye, can disrupt the way email filters and even AI models process text. These characters, meant for obscure language tagging, are being used to split words that trigger security alerts, such as "funding" or "payment." This clever manipulation allows malicious content to slip through defenses, appearing as legitimate messages to users while being fundamentally compromised.

The technique, first observed in early February, involves inserting these non-rendering Unicode characters into otherwise innocuous-looking emails. This creates a "shadow copy" of text that security systems struggle to interpret correctly. By doing so, cybercriminals aim to bypass traditional keyword-based filters and trick unsuspecting recipients into engaging with phishing attempts.

## AI-Era Adaptations in Traditional Attacks

What's particularly alarming is how these advanced techniques, often discussed in the context of AI prompt injection, are being adapted for more traditional phishing operations. Threat actors are proving adept at repurposing cutting-edge evasion methods for broad-scale attacks, demonstrating that the lines between sophisticated AI exploits and everyday spam are blurring. Microsoft noted that the Unicode Tags block, specifically characters from U+E0000 to U+E007F, is the primary area of abuse in these campaigns.

This particular phishing campaign reached a peak in volume over approximately three months, before seeing a significant drop around mid-May. The attackers also exhibited a predictable rhythm, largely ceasing operations on weekends only to ramp up activity again at the start of the work week. This methodical approach underscores the organized and persistent nature of these evolving threats.

## What to Watch

The successful deployment of invisible characters in large-scale phishing efforts suggests that defenders will need to continually refine their detection methods. We can anticipate further innovation in how attackers attempt to obscure their malicious intent, potentially leading to new arms races between security researchers and cybercriminals. Users should remain extra vigilant about unsolicited emails, even those that appear to pass all standard security checks.
