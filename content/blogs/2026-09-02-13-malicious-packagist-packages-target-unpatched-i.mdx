## Hidden Dangers Lurk: Malicious Software Targets Unpatched iPhones for Crypto Theft

Cybersecurity experts have uncovered a sophisticated threat targeting users of popular movie and comic streaming websites. A batch of 13 malicious software packages, masquerading as legitimate design themes, have been found to inject harmful code designed to steal cryptocurrency wallet information from unpatched iPhones. This discovery highlights a persistent and evolving threat landscape that preys on software vulnerabilities.

### The Deceptive Design Packages

These malicious packages, discovered on the Packagist repository, were cleverly disguised as themes for the Composer content management system. When unsuspecting website owners installed these seemingly innocent themes, they inadvertently introduced malicious JavaScript into their sites. This injected code then served a dual purpose: it engaged visitors in ad fraud and redirected them to gambling and adult content sites, while simultaneously launching a more sinister attack specifically aimed at iPhones.

The attack chain, as described by researchers, leverages two critical WebKit vulnerabilities (CVE-2025-31277 and CVE-2025-43529) that have already been patched in recent iOS versions. By exploiting these unpatched flaws, the malware can break free from its restricted sandbox environment and escalate its privileges, ultimately leading to the installation of spyware. This spyware is designed to access and steal sensitive cryptocurrency wallet seed phrases, a critical component for accessing and controlling digital assets.

### A Campaign with Deep Roots

This latest discovery is not an isolated incident but appears to be an escalation of a campaign first identified in March 2026. At that time, researchers observed six malicious packages, also posing as OphimCMS themes, that were engaged in redirecting visitors, stealing website URLs, injecting ads, and serving a secondary payload from compromised infrastructure. This new set of 13 packages, spanning multiple vendor namespaces, indicates a broadening of the attackers' methods and a more targeted approach towards exploiting users' personal information and financial assets. The campaign’s infrastructure, linked to a threat actor known as Funnull, suggests a persistent and organized effort to monetize these exploits.

### What To Watch

The primary concern moving forward is the speed at which users and website administrators patch their systems. Given that the exploited vulnerabilities have known fixes, vigilance and prompt updates are crucial. Furthermore, ongoing monitoring of software repositories for similar deceptive packages will be essential to prevent future infections. Users should also be wary of the legitimacy of website themes and plugins, especially those downloaded from less reputable sources.
