## Berlin Stands Firm Against Cyber Extortionists After Major Data Breach

Berlin's state government is facing a serious cybersecurity crisis, confirming it's the victim of a significant data breach and subsequent extortion attempt. In a defiant stance, city officials have declared they will not yield to the hackers' demands, even as the full extent of the stolen information remains under investigation.

### The Scope of the Attack: More Than Just Data Lost

The cyberattack, which first came to light on August 7th, compromised the city's state administrative network. Subsequent forensic analysis revealed further data exfiltration, particularly from the Department for Mobility, Transport, Climate Protection, and Environment. This outflow occurred between August 7th and August 12th, with the compromised network finally being disconnected on August 14th. While the precise nature and volume of the stolen data are still being determined, officials have acknowledged that personal and other non-public information cannot be ruled out as part of the compromised exfiltrated data.

### Refusal to Pay and Ongoing Investigation

Governing Mayor Kai Wegner has been unequivocal, stating, "The state of Berlin is being blackmailed." In response to this brazen act, the city has mobilized its top law enforcement and security agencies. The state criminal police, public prosecutor's office, and federal security authorities are all actively investigating the incident, though no specific group has yet been officially identified as responsible. However, reports from outlets like Der Spiegel, citing security sources, point to the hacking group Rhysida as the entity claiming responsibility, having reportedly posted details of the breach on their darknet leak site. This claim includes an alarming assertion of having scanned 5.79 terabytes of data, allegedly containing personal information on over 12,000 individuals.

### What To Watch

The immediate focus will be on the ongoing investigation to identify the perpetrators and fully assess the damage caused by the data breach. Berlin's authorities are expected to provide further updates on the types of data compromised and guidance for any citizens whose personal information may have been exposed. The city's firm refusal to pay the ransom will likely be a key factor in how this situation develops, potentially setting a precedent for other municipalities facing similar threats.
