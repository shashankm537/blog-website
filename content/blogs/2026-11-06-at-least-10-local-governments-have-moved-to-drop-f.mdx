## Local Governments Hit Pause on Pervasive Surveillance Tech

A growing wave of unease over data privacy and mass surveillance is prompting a significant number of local governments to reconsider their reliance on advanced AI technology. Since the summer, at least ten municipalities and police departments have either severed ties with, restricted, or initiated the process of dropping Flock Safety's surveillance camera systems, signaling a broader public pushback against the rapid integration of such tools.

### Mounting Concerns Over Flock's Reach

Flock Safety, a company established in 2017, provides a suite of surveillance technologies, including license plate readers, drones, and audio detection devices capable of identifying gunshots. These tools are marketed as vital for crime-solving, assisting in tasks like locating missing persons or recovering stolen vehicles, with over 100,000 of their cameras already deployed across the nation. However, recent months have seen a sharp increase in backlash. Notably, cities such as Monroe, Ohio; Newburgh, New York; Richmond, Virginia; Hopkinton and New Bedford, Massachusetts; and Lansing, Michigan, have all moved to terminate their contracts with Flock. Similarly, police departments in East Bridgewater and Grafton, Massachusetts, have announced their intentions to cease using the cameras. This sentiment even reached the ballot in League City, Texas, where residents voted this past week to remove Flock's license plate readers from their community.

### Privacy and Data Practices Under Scrutiny

The objections raised by these local governments are multifaceted, encompassing significant concerns about individual privacy, the transparency of data sharing protocols, and the methods by which these surveillance systems are procured. In Asheville, North Carolina, police services lost Flock access following a city council vote in August to end the contract. Meanwhile, the El Paso County Sheriff's Office in Colorado announced in September that it would discontinue its use of Flock cameras. Sheriff Joseph Roybal stated that the decision was made to better serve the county, asserting that their existing resources are sufficient for apprehending criminals without relying on Flock's technology. Experts in digital privacy, like Tatiana Rice of The Future of Privacy Forum, have highlighted these growing concerns as a critical factor in the current reevaluation of surveillance technology by local authorities.

### What To Watch

Expect to see more local governments scrutinizing their existing surveillance contracts and potentially implementing stricter policies around data collection and usage. The trend of communities demanding greater transparency and control over the deployment of AI-powered surveillance tools is likely to continue, potentially influencing how companies like Flock Safety operate and engage with the public sector moving forward.
