## Storm in the Cloud: Azure CLI Targeted in Massive Password Spray Attack

Cybersecurity experts are sounding the alarm about a significant and persistent automated attack that has targeted Microsoft's Azure command-line interface (CLI). This sophisticated operation has managed to breach dozens of accounts, highlighting a critical vulnerability in cloud security.

### A Million Miles from Secure: The Scale of the Attack

In a two-week period between June 12 and June 26, a relentless barrage of over 81 million login attempts was unleashed. This massive offensive, traced back to an IPv6 address range managed by internet infrastructure provider LSHIY LLC, successfully compromised at least 78 Microsoft accounts. These compromised accounts belonged to individuals across 64 different organizations, demonstrating the broad reach of this cyber threat. The attackers appear to be employing a brute-force strategy, focusing on commonly used passwords found in leaked credential databases, rather than targeting specific industries.

### Circumventing Defenses: The ROPC Exploit

What makes this attack particularly concerning is its ability to bypass robust security measures, including Conditional Access policies. The threat actor is exploiting a deprecated OAuth 2.0 flow known as Resource Owner Password Credentials (ROPC). This legacy method allows an application to directly handle user credentials, which is precisely what attackers exploited to gain unauthorized access. Microsoft itself has warned against the use of ROPC, noting its incompatibility with multi-factor authentication and recommending more secure alternatives for most scenarios due to the inherent risks.

### What To Watch

The ongoing nature of this attack suggests that defenders are likely still working to fully understand its scope and implement countermeasures. Organizations utilizing Azure CLI should review their authentication methods, prioritize strong, unique passwords, and ensure multi-factor authentication is enforced wherever possible. The continued exploitation of legacy authentication flows like ROPC also serves as a stark reminder for businesses to regularly audit their security configurations and phase out outdated technologies.
