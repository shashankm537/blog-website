# North Korean Hackers Exploit IT Firm to Target Developers with Sophisticated Backdoors

Cybersecurity experts have uncovered a disturbing new campaign by the North Korean hacking group Jade Sleet, which leveraged a breach at an Indian IT services company to deploy advanced malware. This sophisticated operation, aimed at developers, underscores the persistent threat North Korea poses to the global tech landscape and its focus on the lucrative cryptocurrency sector.

## The Jade Sleet Playbook: Exploiting Trust and Technology

The latest findings reveal that Jade Sleet, a notorious threat actor also known by aliases like PUKCHONG and Slow Pisces, has been actively targeting individuals within the information technology sector. SentinelOne, a leading cybersecurity firm, detailed how the group gained access to an unnamed Indian IT provider. From there, they sought to infiltrate broader networks by compromising the developers working for that organization. This tactic of targeting the supply chain, particularly the developers who build and maintain crucial software, has become a hallmark of Jade Sleet's operations.

### Deploying Advanced macOS Backdoors

During this latest breach, Jade Sleet employed two sophisticated backdoors designed for Apple's macOS operating system: FLATROOF and ROOFDECK. These tools are not new to the cybersecurity world; they were previously identified as part of an attack on the KelpDAO LayerZero bridge in early 2026. The use of these advanced backdoors highlights Jade Sleet's technical capabilities and their persistent efforts to maintain access and extract valuable information or assets. Their primary focus remains on organizations and individuals involved in cryptocurrency and blockchain technologies, aiming for significant financial gains.

## Social Engineering: The Human Element of the Attack

A crucial element of Jade Sleet's strategy involves deceptive social engineering tactics. Researchers observed the group using job interview lures as a primary method to ensnare their targets. This often involves creating convincing fake GitHub repositories that mimic real infrastructure engineering projects. Individuals in high-demand fields such as DevOps, cryptocurrency development, and fintech are particularly vulnerable to these elaborate schemes, as they are often actively seeking new opportunities. By posing as legitimate employers, Jade Sleet can trick developers into downloading malicious code disguised as project files.

### Weaponized Code and Supply Chain Risks

The weaponization of the Terraform dependency lock file (".terraform.lock.hcl") within these fake repositories is a particularly alarming aspect of this campaign. This technique allows the attackers to subtly inject malicious code that can be executed when developers work on the project. This sophisticated approach not only compromises individual machines but also creates a pathway for the attackers to infiltrate the internal systems of the breached IT company and, potentially, its clients. The incident serves as a stark reminder of the significant risks associated with supply chain attacks, where a single compromised vendor can have far-reaching consequences.

## What To Watch

The ongoing activity of Jade Sleet, particularly their focus on the cryptocurrency ecosystem and their evolving exploitation methods, warrants close attention. Organizations in the IT and Web3 sectors should remain vigilant, reinforcing their security protocols and employee training on social engineering threats. The continuous development and deployment of advanced macOS malware by state-sponsored actors like Jade Sleet suggest that these sophisticated attacks will likely persist.
