## NSE IPO: When Pre-IPO Dreams Meet Public Market Reality

The much-anticipated Initial Public Offering (IPO) of the National Stock Exchange (NSE) is finally on the horizon, but for some early investors who dabbled in the unlisted market, the upcoming public offering is proving to be a less than stellar return. One investor, Mahesh Gupta, exemplifies this predicament, facing a potential loss on shares acquired at a premium before the official IPO price was set.

### The Unlisted Market Gamble

For years, the NSE has been a hot commodity in India's unlisted stock market, attracting significant interest from a wide array of investors, from large institutions to individual traders. The exchange's dominant position in Indian equities and derivatives fueled high expectations and, consequently, inflated pre-IPO prices. Mahesh Gupta, for instance, purchased NSE shares at Rs 1,826.85 apiece. This price, however, falls above the upper limit of the NSE's IPO price band of Rs 1,785.

### A Reality Check for Early Birds

This discrepancy highlights a critical point for investors participating in the pre-IPO market: the price paid for unlisted shares doesn't guarantee a profit when the company goes public. In Gupta's case, if he sells his 500 shares at the IPO's upper price band, he would experience a price difference of approximately Rs 41.85 per share, totaling a loss of around Rs 20,925. While this might seem like a small percentage (about 2.3%) for a limited number of shares, it serves as a stark reminder that the unlisted market can be akin to a lottery, with no certainty of returns.

### Navigating Valuation and Growth Concerns

The NSE's IPO price band of Rs 1,700-1,785 per share has come in lower than the Rs 2,000-2,100 range many anticipated. This adjusted pricing, according to Ishan Tanna, Senior Associate at Ashika Capital, reflects concerns about valuation and future growth. While the NSE's valuation at around 43 times FY26 earnings might seem reasonable compared to domestic peers like BSE and MCX, it still positions it at a premium to most global exchanges. The crucial question now for investors is how the exchange will sustain its growth trajectory.

### What To Watch

As the NSE IPO progresses, all eyes will be on the final allotment and subsequent trading performance. Investors will be keenly observing whether the market ultimately validates the exchange's valuation and growth prospects, or if the pre-IPO dream for some early investors turns into a hard reality check. The success of this IPO could also set a precedent for other major unlisted entities considering a public debut in India.
