## Yes Bank Takes Legal Battle to Supreme Court Over Rs 8,415 Crore Bond Write-Off

Yes Bank is challenging a recent court decision that overturned its attempt to write off a substantial Rs 8,415 crore in Additional Tier-1 (AT-1) bonds. The bank has now taken its case to the nation's highest court, seeking a review of an order that favored bondholders. This legal maneuver signals a significant escalation in the ongoing dispute surrounding the bank's financial restructuring.

### The High Court's Ruling and Yes Bank's Disagreement

The core of the issue stems from a Bombay High Court ruling on October 15th, which sided with investors. The High Court declared Yes Bank's decision to write off these particular AT-1 bonds invalid, effectively quashing the bank's action. The bank had argued that these write-offs were a necessary step during a period of severe financial distress, intended to bolster its capital reserves. However, the High Court found that the administrator responsible for the bank at the time lacked the proper authority to implement such a drastic measure.

### A Legal Escalation to the Apex Court

Following the unfavorable ruling from the Bombay High Court, Yes Bank has now formally lodged a petition with the Supreme Court of India. The bank is requesting a thorough review of the High Court's order, aiming to have it overturned. This move underscores the bank's commitment to its position that the write-off was a legitimate and necessary action taken under challenging circumstances. The ultimate outcome of this Supreme Court challenge could have significant implications for both the bank and the broader landscape of AT-1 bond regulations in India.

### What To Watch

Investors and financial analysts will be closely watching the Supreme Court's proceedings. The court's decision could set a precedent for how AT-1 bond write-offs are handled in future financial crises. It will be crucial to observe the arguments presented by both Yes Bank and the bondholders, as well as the legal interpretations that guide the judges' final verdict. The timeline for this review is currently unclear, but the matter is expected to be a significant point of interest in the Indian financial news cycle.
