## Foreign Investors Exit Indian Equities in a Torrent: What's Driving the Selloff?

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have intensified their selloff of Indian stocks, offloading a staggering Rs 45,000 crore in just the first six trading sessions of October. This dramatic exodus mirrors the net selling seen throughout the entire month of September and signals a significant shift in investor sentiment towards the Indian market. Despite a recent brief respite in the market's losing streak, foreign funds have remained resolutely bearish, consistently selling for over a fortnight.

### The Gravity of Rising Bond Yields

A primary driver behind this rapid FII withdrawal appears to be the surging yields on global bonds. As risk-free returns from debt instruments become increasingly attractive, the allure of equities diminishes. Notably, the 10-year US Treasury yield has climbed to multi-year highs, even surpassing its 2007 peak and reaching levels not seen since 2002. This makes investments in sovereign debt a more appealing, less risky proposition for global investors compared to the volatility of equity markets.

### The Cumulative Impact on Indian Markets

This sustained FII selling spree has had a profound impact on Indian equities. Analysts point out that the aggressive outflows are a major contributor to the Indian market's underperformance this year, with the Nifty index registering negative returns. For foreign investors, the opportunity to earn over 5.2% in risk-free returns from 10-year US bonds presents a compelling alternative to navigating the complexities and risks associated with emerging markets. The sheer volume of selling indicates a strategic reallocation of capital by these large institutional players.

### What To Watch

The immediate focus will be on whether global bond yields stabilize or continue their upward trajectory, as this will significantly influence FII sentiment. Any positive developments on the inflation front or signals of a less aggressive stance from central banks could potentially ease the pressure. Investors will also be closely monitoring corporate earnings and domestic economic indicators for signs of strength that might lure foreign capital back into the Indian market.
