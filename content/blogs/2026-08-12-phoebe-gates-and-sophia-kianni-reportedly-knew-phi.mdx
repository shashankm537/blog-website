## Startup Co-Founded by Gates & Kianni Faces Renewed Scrutiny Over Alleged Deceptive Practices

A shopping startup co-founded by Phoebe Gates and Sophia Kianni is once again at the center of controversy, with new reports suggesting the company, Phia, has been engaging in potentially misleading business tactics for months. This latest wave of allegations concerns a practice known as "cookie stuffing," which the company reportedly knew about long before publicly acknowledging it.

### A Deeper Dive into "Cookie Stuffing"

At its core, cookie stuffing involves a platform claiming credit and commission for sales it didn't actually facilitate. In the world of affiliate marketing, this is a significant issue as it diverts revenue away from legitimate marketers and businesses that genuinely drove the customer to the purchase. Startups like Phia typically agree in their contracts to a strict ban on such practices, as it's widely considered an unfair competitive tactic. The implications for Phia are serious, with the potential for legal repercussions and damage to its reputation within the industry.

### Executives Reportedly Aware of "Bug" for Months

Previously, when these allegations first surfaced earlier this year, a Phia spokesperson stated the company was unaware of the cookie stuffing until contacted by investigators. However, new reporting from Bloomberg, based on leaked internal communications and sources close to the company, paints a different picture. These revelations suggest that Phoebe Gates and Sophia Kianni were aware of the cookie stuffing practice as far back as December, with internal messages indicating discussions about the strategy with other executives and engineers. The practice reportedly constituted a substantial portion of Phia's sales, with a notable revenue dip occurring once the activity ceased. Retail giants like Nike and Nordstrom are among the affected businesses.

### From "Bug" to Feature: Shifting Narratives

Further complicating matters is the recharacterization of the issue. While Phia's representatives initially described the cookie stuffing as a technical "bug" in early July, recent reports indicate it was, in fact, a deliberately engineered feature that could be activated and deactivated. Leaked Slack messages reportedly show the co-founders directly involved in discussions about this practice. When confronted with the new allegations, a Phia spokesperson acknowledged some discrepancies but stated the company would "learn from this." The company also issued a statement confirming that any features causing misattributions were removed over a month prior, on July 7.

### What To Watch

The ongoing investigation and the company's responses will be crucial. The extent of the alleged deception and the clarity of Phia's commitment to ethical practices will determine the long-term impact on the startup and its founders. The involvement of major retailers also suggests a potential for broader industry scrutiny.
