## HDFC Bank: ICICI Securities Sees Upside Ahead, Upgrades to 'Buy'

Following a recent significant merger, analysts at ICICI Securities are signaling optimism for HDFC Bank, recommending a "Buy" and setting a target price of Rs 1,850. This bullish outlook stems from a detailed analysis of the bank's post-merger financial adjustments and growth prospects.

### Navigating the Post-Merger Landscape

The integration of HDFC into HDFC Bank has naturally led to shifts in the bank's financial structure. Key changes include a notable rise in the loan-to-deposit ratio (LDR) and an increase in borrowing, while the share of low-cost CASA deposits has seen a decrease. Despite these adjustments, the merger has significantly bolstered HDFC Bank's loan market share, adding 330 basis points to reach 14.5%. Deposit growth, while slower at 110 basis points year-on-year to 11.0%, is still projected to be robust. ICICI Securities forecasts an impressive 18% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) for deposits between FY24 and FY26, translating to an absolute increase of Rs 4 to 5 trillion annually.

### Growth Projections and Revised Estimates

Looking ahead, ICICI Securities anticipates HDFC Bank will focus on achieving a balanced LDR and managing its borrowing mix. Consequently, loan growth estimates have been adjusted to a 13% CAGR for FY24-26, with the LDR expected to ease to 100% and 96% by FY25 and FY26, respectively, down from the current 104%. This implies an incremental LDR of 74-75%. While the fourth quarter of FY24 saw a slightly better-than-expected net interest margin (NIM), it's believed to be partly influenced by seasonal factors. Based on these growth projections, EPS estimates for FY25 and FY26 have been revised downward by 5%. Despite this adjustment, the bank is still expected to deliver a healthy return on assets (RoA) of 1.7% and a return on equity (RoE) of 14-15% for FY25-26.

### What To Watch

With the stock price having experienced a notable correction of 10% year-to-date, ICICI Securities believes this presents an opportune moment for investors. The upgrade to "Buy" suggests that the current valuation may not fully reflect the bank's long-term potential. Investors will be keenly observing how effectively HDFC Bank manages its deposit accretion and credit growth in the coming quarters, alongside its strategy for borrowing substitution, as these will be key drivers influencing its overall growth trajectory and net interest margins. The gradual re-rating of the stock will likely depend on these factors, even with strong underlying deposit growth.
