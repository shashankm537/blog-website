## AI Security: Even Google is Navigating the Rapids in Real Time

The rapid ascent of Artificial Intelligence has thrust security concerns into the spotlight, forcing organizations worldwide to grapple with new vulnerabilities. Even tech giants like Google are in a constant state of adaptation, as revealed in a recent conversation with Francis de Souza, COO of Google Cloud. His insights offer a clear roadmap for businesses embarking on their AI journey, emphasizing that security must be woven into the fabric of innovation, not treated as an add-on.

### Security as a Foundation, Not an Afterthought

De Souza stressed a critical point that has long eluded many executives: security cannot be an afterthought in the age of AI. "Companies need to take a platform approach," he advised, meaning that security, governance, and auditability must be considered from the very inception of any AI initiative. This includes actively combating "shadow AI," where employees bypass official channels to use unvetted consumer tools, potentially exposing sensitive data. He made it unequivocally clear that an AI strategy is inseparable from a robust data and security strategy; these pillars must advance in tandem.

### Embracing a Multicloud Reality

Contrary to what one might expect, de Souza wasn't solely advocating for Google Cloud. He emphasized that the company is committed to a multicloud philosophy, recognizing that few businesses truly operate in a single cloud environment. Even organizations that select a primary cloud provider often utilize various Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) applications and interact with business partners who leverage different cloud infrastructures. This interconnectedness necessitates a consistent and comprehensive security posture that can span across diverse cloud platforms and AI models, ensuring protection regardless of where data resides or is processed.

### The Evolving Threat Landscape Demands Agility

The nature of cyber threats has been fundamentally reshaped by AI. De Souza highlighted that traditional, often slow-moving, defensive models are no longer sufficient to counter the speed and sophistication of modern attacks. The window between an initial breach and its full exploitation has dramatically narrowed, demanding a proactive and agile approach to cybersecurity. This shift requires organizations to move beyond reactive measures and build resilient security frameworks that can adapt to the constantly evolving threat landscape.

### What To Watch

Expect to see a continued focus on integrated security solutions that offer broad visibility and control across hybrid and multicloud environments. Businesses will likely prioritize platforms that provide built-in governance and auditability features for AI deployments. Furthermore, the industry will be watching how companies balance the rapid adoption of AI with the critical need for robust security protocols, and how this evolving dynamic impacts the competitive landscape.
