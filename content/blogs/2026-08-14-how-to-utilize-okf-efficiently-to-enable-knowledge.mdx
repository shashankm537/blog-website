## AI Agents Just Got Smarter: Sharing Brainpower for Faster Results

Imagine a team of AI assistants, each an expert in a different area, working together seamlessly. Google's latest innovation, a clever adaptation of their Open Knowledge Format (OKF), is making this a reality, significantly speeding up how these artificial intelligences communicate and collaborate. This new method allows different AI models, even those with slightly different architectures, to efficiently share crucial information, leading to a dramatic reduction in processing time.

### A Smarter Way to Share Knowledge

At its core, this advancement involves transforming a standard Markdown file into a sophisticated communication tool for AI agents. Google's Open Knowledge Format, typically used for organizing information, has been enhanced with a special "token_pointer" field. This field doesn't just describe information; it provides a direct, machine-readable link to pre-computed data stored in shared memory, essentially giving AI agents a shortcut to essential processed knowledge. This human-readable document structure combined with a machine-optimized pointer streamlines the hand-off process between different AI models.

### Turbocharging AI Teamwork

The real magic happens when multiple AI models, like various sizes of Google's Qwen2.5-Coder, need to work together. While these models might not be able to share their internal "memory" (the KV cache) due to architectural differences, they can absolutely share the initial processing steps. By pre-tokenizing input data once and storing it as an array of numerical IDs, this system allows subsequent AI agents to bypass their own tokenization process entirely. This is possible because the entire Qwen2.5-Coder family uses the same vocabulary, ensuring compatibility and eliminating redundant work.

### Measurable Gains in Speed

The impact of this shared tokenization strategy is substantial. In tests, the time it takes for AI models to generate their first output (Time To First Token, or TTFT) saw significant improvements. For the 3 billion parameter Qwen2.5-Coder model, TTFT dropped by approximately 28%, from 69.3 milliseconds to 49.9 milliseconds. The smaller 1.5 billion parameter model experienced an even more impressive 37.8% reduction, falling from 49.6 ms to 30.9 ms. These are not minor tweaks; they represent a tangible leap in efficiency for AI workflows.

### What To Watch

This breakthrough signals a new era for multi-agent AI systems, where efficiency and collaboration are paramount. Expect to see further refinements in how AI models share intermediate results and potentially new formats designed to optimize these complex interactions. The focus will likely be on reducing latency and increasing the overall throughput of sophisticated AI pipelines.
