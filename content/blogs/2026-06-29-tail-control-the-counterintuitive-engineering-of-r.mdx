## The Unseen Struggle: Making AI Work Reliably for Your Customers

When the stakes are high and a customer's operation hinges on your service, even minor AI hiccups can cascade into major disruptions. This isn't about internal debugging; it's about delivering a flawless, timely result to an external partner where "good enough" simply won't cut it. At Databook, where we process vast amounts of data for global enterprises, we've learned some critical lessons about building AI workflows that actually work, not just in theory, but in real-world, high-pressure scenarios.

### The Four Horsemen of AI Failure

The core challenge lies in the inherent unpredictability of Large Language Models (LLMs). They don't just get things wrong; they can falter in several distinct ways. An LLM might provide an answer that's completely unparseable, factually incorrect, or even just an empty void. Then there are outright hard errors that halt the entire process, or the agonizing silence of no response at all. Perhaps the most insidious failure, however, is the one that arrives too late; it might appear successful on your end, but from the customer's perspective, it's a failure because their process couldn't wait any longer. When you string multiple LLM calls together, each step introduces a new point of potential failure, turning an otherwise robust sequence into a fragile chain.

### When Internal Slack Disappears

Within your own company, a slow or failed process is often manageable. You can retry, wait a bit longer, or even adjust your expectations. However, when that same workflow is exposed via an API to a customer, all that internal flexibility vanishes. You're now operating under strict external constraints, primarily around time. Customers have hard timeouts – connections severed after a couple of minutes – and Service Level Agreements (SLAs) that dictate how long they can afford to wait. This means your AI workflow isn't just competing against its own internal logic; it's racing against a ticking clock that you don't control. If that window closes, the customer has no choice but to restart the entire process, and your previous efforts become irrelevant.

### What To Watch

Expect to see a growing focus on sophisticated error handling and retry mechanisms specifically designed for LLM-based workflows. The development of more robust LLM architectures that offer greater predictability and faster response times will also be key. Furthermore, companies will likely invest more in robust monitoring and alerting systems to detect those "quiet" failures, like late responses, before they impact customers.
