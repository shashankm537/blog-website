**SEBI Signals Easing Rules for Small Debt Placements, Aiming to Boost Capital Access**

In a move that could streamline fundraising for smaller ventures, India's securities market regulator, SEBI, has proposed to remove the mandatory requirement of appointing a merchant banker for certain small-value debt issuances through private placements. This potential change aims to significantly reduce compliance burdens and encourage more companies to tap into the debt markets for capital.

### Simplifying the Path to Capital for Smaller Issuances

Currently, companies looking to raise funds through private placements of debt securities or non-convertible redeemable preference shares with a face value of Rs 10,000 are required to engage a merchant banker. SEBI acknowledges that this rule can inflate the cost of capital for issuers, making smaller, more frequent issuances economically unviable and potentially deterring them altogether. By proposing an exemption for "small-value debt," the regulator seeks to create a more accessible and cost-effective avenue for businesses to secure necessary funding.

### Safeguards to Maintain Market Integrity

While easing the process, SEBI is not compromising on investor protection. The proposed exemption comes with several crucial conditions designed to ensure the robustness of the market. To qualify, an issuer must be registered or regulated by a financial sector regulator and have a listing history on a recognized stock exchange for at least a year. Furthermore, stock exchanges will be tasked with verifying that companies seeking this exemption have no outstanding penalties or fines from regulators or the exchanges themselves.

Additional stipulations include a clean track record regarding financial obligations. Issuers must demonstrate no defaults in the repayment of deposits, interest on deposits, redemption of preference shares or debt securities, interest on these instruments, dividend payments, or any other financial commitments over the last three financial years and the current fiscal year. These measures are intended to strike a balance between facilitating fundraising and maintaining market discipline and trust.

### What To Watch

Investors and market participants will be keenly observing the feedback received on SEBI's consultation paper. The regulator's final decision on these proposed changes could significantly impact the accessibility of capital for a wider range of companies. We can expect further clarifications and potential adjustments to the conditions before any formal implementation, offering a clearer picture of the new fundraising landscape.
