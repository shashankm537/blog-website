## South Korea's AI Chip Tussle Sparks Market Re-evaluation: India Poised for Gains?

A recent regulatory shake-up in South Korea has sent ripples through the booming artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor market. After months of impressive gains, investors are now taking a closer look at valuations, as the crackdown signals a potential cooling-off period for an overheated sector. While this has hit Korean chipmakers, some market watchers see it as a necessary adjustment that could pave the way for healthier growth.

### Navigating the AI Semiconductor Frenzy

The intense focus on AI has driven significant investor interest, particularly in leveraged exchange-traded funds heavily weighted towards major players like Samsung and SK Hynix. Rahul Chadha, speaking from Shikhara Investment, noted that the semiconductor sector had become "frothy," with his own funds maintaining a cautious underweight position. He suggests that a modest pullback in share prices is a healthy development, allowing for a more rational assessment of future prospects.

The critical question for the AI investment narrative, according to Chadha, lies with the "Magnificent Seven" tech giants. Their future capital expenditure plans for AI infrastructure will be a key indicator of sustained demand. A slight decline in semiconductor stocks might continue, but any significant impact on the broader AI trade will depend on shifts in demand or potential pullbacks in spending by these major tech companies.

### The Long Road to Chip Supply Normalization

Despite the current adjustments, the fundamental drivers for AI semiconductors remain robust. Persistent chip shortages have led to exceptionally strong earnings revisions, a trend that is expected to continue for some time. Estimates suggest that the supply-demand imbalance in the semiconductor market might not fully resolve until 2028, implying continued underlying demand for the foreseeable future. Beyond the AI chip dynamics, broader market sentiment in South Korea is also being influenced by discussions around taxing unrealized gains and the country's recent failure to achieve developed market status from MSCI, partly due to ongoing currency convertibility challenges.

### What To Watch

Investors will be closely monitoring the quarterly earnings reports of major AI chip manufacturers and semiconductor equipment suppliers for any signs of softening demand or changes in capital expenditure guidance. Additionally, any further regulatory developments in South Korea or shifts in global currency policies could impact market sentiment. The performance of India's own technology and manufacturing sectors, particularly those benefiting from supply chain diversification, will also be a key area of focus as the global AI landscape evolves.
