**IT Stocks Tumble: Nifty IT Hits Three-Year Low as Global Headwinds Bite**

Indian IT stocks experienced a significant downturn on Friday, with the Nifty IT index plummeting over 6% to its lowest point in three years. This sharp decline was triggered by a cautious outlook from global IT giant Accenture, which sent shockwaves through the market and led to substantial losses for major Indian players like Infosys and HCL Tech.

### Accenture's Shadow Looms Large

The catalyst for the sell-off appears to be Accenture's revised revenue growth forecast, which was cut to 3-4% for fiscal year 2026, down from its previous projection of 3-5%. Furthermore, the company's fourth-quarter revenue projections fell short of market expectations. This downward revision signals a potential slowdown in client spending on IT consulting and digital transformation projects, a concern for Indian IT firms that heavily rely on the U.S. market.

### Market Reaction and Investor Concerns

Following Accenture's news, shares of Indian IT majors saw immediate pressure. Infosys was among the hardest hit, with its stock crashing by nearly 9%. Other prominent companies like TCS, HCL Tech, and LTI Mindtree also experienced significant drops, ranging from 4% to 6%. This broad-based decline underscores the interconnectedness of the global IT services sector and the impact of even a single major player's sentiment on the broader market. Investors are now grappling with concerns about reduced discretionary spending by enterprises, even as investments in areas like artificial intelligence and cybersecurity remain robust.

### What To Watch

The market will be closely watching for any further commentary from Indian IT companies regarding their outlook for the coming quarters. Investors will also be keen to see if any signs of stabilization or potential recovery emerge in Accenture's stock and within the broader global IT sector. The question of whether this steep correction presents a "buy the dip" opportunity will likely remain a central debate among analysts and investors in the short term.
