## Ex-OpenAI Engineer Denies Secret Recording Charges, Faces U.S. Court

A former engineer at the artificial intelligence giant OpenAI has formally entered a plea of not guilty in federal court, denying charges that he planted a clandestine recording device within a company meeting room. The development follows his arrest last month amid allegations of an attempt to unlawfully capture sensitive internal discussions.

### The Allegations Unveiled

Prosecutors in San Francisco have accused Saudi national Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, of orchestrating a scheme to record confidential conversations at OpenAI. The indictment details the alleged placement of a "sophisticated recording device" within an executive conference room, a move described as a deliberate effort to intercept sensitive discussions. Alfozan, who has been held without bail since his October 9th arrest, appeared in court on Friday to enter his not guilty plea.

### A Swift Departure and Courtroom Proceedings

The arrest of Alfozan occurred just days before he, his wife, and their son were slated to depart the U.S. for Saudi Arabia on a one-way ticket. Court documents reveal a complex series of travel arrangements for his family, with his wife and son ultimately leaving the country shortly after his apprehension, reportedly booked on flights paid for by the Saudi government. Alfozan's next court date is set for November 17, 2026, and if convicted on the single charge of interception of oral communications, he faces a maximum of five years in prison and a $250,000 fine.

### What To Watch

The upcoming court proceedings will likely focus on the evidence presented regarding the alleged device and Alfozan's alleged communications with Saudi officials. His status as a flight risk, highlighted by his family's departure, will also undoubtedly be a significant factor as the case progresses towards trial or potential plea negotiations.
