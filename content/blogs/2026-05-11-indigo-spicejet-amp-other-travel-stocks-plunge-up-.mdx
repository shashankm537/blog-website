# Travel Stocks Take a Hit as PM Advises Against Foreign Trips

A significant downturn swept through India's travel sector on Monday, with major players like IndiGo and SpiceJet experiencing sharp declines of up to 7%. This market reaction followed a direct appeal from Prime Minister Narendra Modi urging citizens to put a pause on non-essential international travel for at least the next year, a move that has sent ripples of concern through the industry.

## The Prime Minister's Call to Action

Prime Minister Modi's address to the nation this past weekend carried a pointed message: in the face of global uncertainties, a collective resolution towards austerity is needed. Specifically, he encouraged a significant reduction in the use of petrol and diesel, drawing parallels to the nation's adaptability during the COVID-19 pandemic. The suggestion to re-embrace remote working, virtual meetings, and online conferences, while beneficial for reducing fuel consumption related to travel, has directly impacted the outlook for airlines and travel booking platforms. This advisory, aimed at conserving fuel and bolstering national economic resilience, has cast a shadow over the immediate future of outbound tourism and business travel.

## Rising Oil Prices Add to the Woes

Adding fuel to the fire, or rather, to the airlines' operational costs, is the persistent surge in crude oil prices. Following a brief dip, global oil prices have again climbed, breaching the $105 per barrel mark as diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions in the West Asia faltered. For airlines, aviation turbine fuel (ATF) constitutes a substantial portion of their operating expenses. Increased ATF costs translate directly into higher operational overheads, squeezing profit margins for carriers like IndiGo and SpiceJet, particularly when the ability to immediately pass these costs onto consumers through higher ticket prices is limited.

## What To Watch

Investors and industry analysts will be closely monitoring the government's next steps and any potential measures to support the affected travel sector. The sustained impact of high crude oil prices on airline profitability remains a key concern, as does the public's adherence to the Prime Minister's advisory on foreign travel. The performance of travel booking platforms will also be a bellwether for consumer confidence and discretionary spending in the coming months.
