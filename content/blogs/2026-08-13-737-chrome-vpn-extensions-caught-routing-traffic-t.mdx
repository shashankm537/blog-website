## Danger Lurks in Your Browser: Thousands of Fake VPN Extensions Exposed

In a concerning revelation for internet users, researchers have uncovered a massive network of over 700 free VPN and proxy extensions in the Chrome Web Store. These seemingly helpful tools, designed to bypass online restrictions, were instead secretly routing user traffic through a single proxy provider, potentially exposing sensitive data.

### The Trojan Horse of Online Privacy

The sheer scale of this operation is alarming. A staggering 737 extensions, disguised as legitimate privacy tools, were found to be compromising user security. Many of these fake extensions even went so far as to impersonate well-known and trusted VPN brands like NordVPN, Surfshark, and ExpressVPN, tricking unsuspecting users into installing them. With over 75,000 installations recorded, a significant number of people may have inadvertently placed their online activity at risk.

### A Sophisticated Scheme to Intercept Data

Security researchers have detailed how these malicious extensions operate. Instead of providing a secure tunnel for your internet traffic, they reroute your entire browsing session through a network of SOCKS5 proxies controlled by a single entity. This setup effectively places the threat actor in a prime position to observe everything you do online, including the websites you visit, your source IP address, and any unencrypted data you transmit. The extensions are designed to funnel all traffic, except for essential local network communication, through this compromised proxy.

### What to Watch

While some of these malicious extensions have been removed from the Chrome Web Store, hundreds remain active. Users are strongly advised to review their installed Chrome extensions and remove any that appear suspicious or are from unknown developers, especially those claiming to be free VPNs. Vigilance is key to protecting your online privacy from these deceptive threats.
