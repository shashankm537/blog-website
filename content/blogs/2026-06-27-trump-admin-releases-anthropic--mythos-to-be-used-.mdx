## AI Tensions Ease: Trump Administration Relaxes Ban on Key Cybersecurity Models

After a swift, two-week ban that removed powerful AI tools from public access, the Trump administration is signaling a shift in its approach. Cybersecurity-focused AI models, specifically Anthropic's Mythos 5, are now being made available to a select group of over 100 U.S. government agencies and companies. This move also includes a notable allowance for non-American employees within these organizations to access the technology.

### Key Access Restored for Critical Infrastructure

The initial restriction on Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models, aimed at preventing potential misuse, has seen a significant amendment. The latest directive specifically permits the deployment of Mythos 5 to trusted partners operating and defending critical U.S. infrastructure. This includes allowing non-U.S. citizens working for these designated entities, as well as Anthropic's own international staff, to regain access to the advanced cybersecurity model. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick confirmed the decision, stating that adequate safeguards are now in place for these privileged users.

### Safeguards and the Fate of Fable 5

The reintroduction of Mythos 5 comes after concerns were raised about the ease with which security researchers had reportedly bypassed its protective guardrails. While the administration has addressed Mythos 5, the directive does not appear to include the Fable 5 model. This version was widely distributed just prior to the ban, on the premise that it offered enhanced security features. The current focus appears to be on carefully reintroducing the most potent cybersecurity tool to entities deemed critical for national security and defense.

### What To Watch

The coming weeks will likely see the full reintegration of Mythos 5 into the operations of the authorized U.S. organizations. Attention will also be keenly focused on any further developments regarding the Fable 5 model, and whether similar access will be granted. Additionally, observers will be monitoring the long-term implications of this selective access policy on the broader landscape of AI development and deployment.
