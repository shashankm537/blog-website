# Synology NAS Devices Under Fire: Ransomware Exploits Older Software Flaws

**Data security is paramount, and Synology is sounding the alarm as cybercriminals are actively targeting its Network Attached Storage (NAS) devices. Attackers are leveraging a previously patched security vulnerability to encrypt user data and demand ransom, putting the sensitive information of businesses and individuals at risk.**

## The Exploited Weakness: A Ghost from the Past

Synology has confirmed that attackers are exploiting a security flaw that was actually fixed in December of last year. This means that devices running older versions of Synology's DiskStation Manager (DSM) software are particularly vulnerable. The attackers' goal is clear: to lock down your data and then demand payment for its release. While Synology hasn't revealed the specific vulnerability, their advisory points to a cluster of issues that, if unpatched, could allow unauthorized access and control.

## Critical Flaws Left Unaddressed

The security advisories highlight several concerning vulnerabilities, two of which carried a severe CVSS score of 9.8. These critical flaws in the system plugin daemon of DSM could have allowed attackers to execute malicious code remotely. Another vulnerability, with a CVSS score of 5.3, could have enabled attackers to read sensitive files. The fact that these were patched in late 2024, yet are still being exploited, underscores the importance of keeping software updated. Synology has provided specific version numbers for DSM that users need to upgrade to, ensuring they are protected against these lingering threats.

## What To Watch

The situation demands immediate attention from all Synology NAS users. It is crucial to check your DSM version and upgrade to the latest available patch as soon as possible. Stay informed about any further advisories from Synology, and consider implementing additional security measures such as strong passwords, disabling unnecessary services, and regular data backups to mitigate potential losses.
